#### Copy:
> Over 40% of freelance invoices face payment delays due to minor formatting and administrative errors.
> 
> If you work with international or corporate clients, here are 5 quick checks before sending your next invoice:
> 
> 1. **Explicit Payment Terms**: Don't write "Due Soon." Specify "Net 15" or a calendar date like "Due: November 15, 2026."
> 2. **Clear Currency Specification**: Writing "\$1,000" causes confusion between USD, CAD, and AUD. Always write "USD \$1,000" or your exact ISO code.
> 3. **Itemized Scope**: Group deliverables with quantities or hours so finance teams don't have to cross-check old email threads.
> 4. **Tax / Registration Identifiers**: If billing in the UK, EU, or Canada, include your VAT/GST number or note your tax exemption status.
> 5. **Bank / Wire Details on the PDF**: Never assume clients have your routing or SWIFT numbers saved from a previous email.

#### Copy:
> True story from a marketing lead: A company printed 10,000 event badges with a customized QR code. 
> 
> On the day of the conference, badge lanyards and lighting glare caused over 30% of scans to fail at check-in.
> 
> Why? The QR code had been exported with Level 'L' (Low) error correction.
> 
> Quick technical guide for anyone generating QR codes for print:
> 
> 📌 **Level L (7% recovery)**: Best for tiny digital icons on pristine screens with zero obstruction.  
> 📌 **Level M (15% recovery)**: Standard default for digital menus.  
> 📌 **Level Q (25% recovery)**: Great for outdoor posters exposed to direct sunlight or mild wear.  
> 📌 **Level H (30% recovery)**: Critical if you plan to overlay a brand logo in the center or print on curved packaging.
